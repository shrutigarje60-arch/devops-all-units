# Focus on User Outcomes: Enterprise Design Thinking Notes

> "Outcome-based thinking is the discipline of defining success as a change in human behavior rather than the completion of a task."

---

## 1. The Shift: Outputs vs. Outcomes

| Metric | Output-Centric (Traditional) | Outcome-Focused (Modern) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The things we produce (Code, PDFs, Features). | The result of using the product (Time saved, stress reduced). |
| **Success Criteria** | Was it delivered on time and on budget? | Did the user's life get better? |
| **Risk** | Shipping "junk" that no one uses. | Solving the wrong human problem. |

---

## 2. Empathy as a Strategic Tool

To focus on outcomes, product teams must use immersive research techniques to uncover root human needs:

* **Observational Shadowing:** Watch users in their natural habitat to discover actual behavior rather than stated desires.
* **The 5 Whys:** Repeatedly ask "Why?" to move past surface-level feature requests to the emotional/functional root need.
* **Persona Development:** Focus on **psychographics** (fears, motivations) rather than just standard demographics.

---

## 3. The "Hill" Framework

Requirements should be structured using the 3-part Hill framework:

1. **The Who:** A specific persona *(e.g., Sarah, the stressed Payroll Manager)*
2. **The What:** A specific goal to achieve *(e.g., Process end-of-month payments)*
3. **The Wow:** The differentiator or outcome *(e.g., In under 15 minutes without IT support)*

---

## 4. Key Success Metrics

* **Task Completion Rate:** Can users successfully accomplish their goal?
* **Net Promoter Score (NPS):** Do users value the experience enough to recommend it?
* **Churn Rate:** Are users continuing to stay with the product?
* **Efficiency Gains:** How much time or money did the user save?

---

## 5. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

* **Feature Creep:** Adding unnecessary features that obscure the primary outcome.
* **Confusing Business vs. User Outcomes:** "Increasing revenue" is a business goal; "Getting home in time for dinner" is the user outcome that drives it.
* **The "Loudest Voice" Bias:** Building for demanding stakeholders instead of struggling users.
