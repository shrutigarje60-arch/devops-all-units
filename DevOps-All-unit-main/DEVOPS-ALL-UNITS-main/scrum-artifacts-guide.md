# Scrum Artifacts: Detailed Guide

Scrum artifacts represent work or value and are designed to maximize the transparency of key information. Each formal artifact includes a specific **commitment** to provide focus and a measurable baseline for progress.

---

## 1. The Product Backlog
An emergent, ordered list of everything needed to improve the product. It serves as the single source of work for the Scrum Team.

* **Key Characteristics:**
  * **Dynamic:** Never complete; continuously evolves as long as the product exists.
  * **Ordered:** Prioritized by the Product Owner based on value, risk, and necessity.
  * **Refinement:** Continuous breakdown of items into smaller, precise user stories.
* **Commitment:** ** Product Goal**
  * Describes a future target state of the product for long-term planning and vision.

---

## 2. The Sprint Backlog
A real-time picture of the work planned by Developers for the current Sprint, encompassing the **Why** (Sprint Goal), **What** (selected backlog items), and **How** (actionable delivery plan).

* **Key Characteristics:**
  * **Owned by Developers:** Only Developers modify the Sprint Backlog during an active Sprint.
  * **Real-Time Visibility:** Transparent visualization of ongoing progress.
  * **Evolutionary:** Continuously updated as new insights emerge during execution.
* **Commitment:** ** Sprint Goal**
  * The single, unifying objective for the Sprint that fosters teamwork and focus.

---

## 3. Supplementary Tool: The Burndown Chart
While not a formal Scrum artifact in the official guide, it is an essential visual tool for tracking Sprint Backlog completion.

* **Chart Elements:**
  * **X-Axis:** Sprint timeline (Days).
  * **Y-Axis:** Remaining effort (Story Points or Hours).
  * **Ideal Line:** Linear benchmark indicating steady task completion.
  * **Actual Line:** Real-time completion progress.
* **Interpretation:**
  * *Actual Line above Ideal Line:* Team is **behind schedule**.
  * *Actual Line below Ideal Line:* Team is **ahead of schedule**.
